Nice to meet you, I am Gadi Levy

My name is Gadi Levy, born in 1950, a resident of Zichron Yaakov since 1994, and a 10th-generation Israeli. I am lovingly married to Aviva, father to Michal, Rachel, and Eran, and a proud grandfather to my grandchildren in Israel and abroad.

My connection to materials and creation began very early. When I was only 5 years old, in kindergarten, I created a tiny puppy out of clay. At the age of 12, I carved a small bird out of wood. My dear parents kept these early creations, and they proudly accompany me to this day—a living testimony to the artistic spark that was waiting inside.

For decades, my life path was completely distant from the classical art world. I am a mechanical engineer, a 1977 graduate of the Technion. I served for 25 years in the regular army, in the Ordnance Corps, and after retiring from the IDF with the rank of Lieutenant Colonel, I managed manufacturing plants in various industries— from metal and automotive, through medical and concrete, to the wood industry.

About 15 years ago, following a retirement due to medical reasons, a transformation occurred. Industrial raw materials and engineering calculations made way, and the artist within me burst out with full force. Suddenly, all the technical knowledge, material understanding, and precision I had acquired as an engineer merged with the passion for creation.

My current journey actually began out of longing and love for my young grandchildren overseas. I wanted to give them something unique, so I started creating wooden puzzles for them. My greatest excitement was hearing them proudly tell their friends abroad: "Grandpa made this!".

Since I came to art on my own, I also developed my own unique working method. My "studio" is replaced by a wooden gazebo that I built with my own hands in my backyard, featuring a strong workbench. There, under the open sky, the magic happens: I do not carve in the traditional way. I perform the initial heavy work using a chainsaw and a jigsaw. From there, I move on to dozens of hours of meticulous manual work with a hammer and chisels of various sizes, and I finish with patient polishing — first with hand files, and later with tiny mills mounted on an electric rotary tool.

From one sculpture to the next, I felt my technique, volume, and professional level rise and deepen. Alongside dozens of wood sculptures, I also create oil paintings, which sometimes become unique puzzles, and large pictures (some of which currently adorn my son-in-law's office in America).

After exhibiting my works as part of the "Open Houses" in Zichron Yaakov and having the privilege of sharing my creation with hundreds of visitors, I am thrilled to open the doors of this digital gallery to you.

"Artistry crafted in wood, capturing imagination, geometry, and a lifetime of stories."

Woman's Head Sculpture Dedicated to Breast Cancer Survivors

Handcrafted in the backyard gazebo | Wood type: Pine

During the sculpting process, when I reached the chest, I realized there wasn't enough material. On the breast stump, I inscribed the words "I am a survivor" and carved the breast cancer awareness ribbon, which years later also became the symbol for the hostages.

The Man Torn Between His Desires

Created from Indian Rosewood (Sissoo) that naturally split. The crack inspired the idea of a man debating between his good and evil inclinations. I placed an angel on his right shoulder and a demon on his left shoulder to represent these inner drives.

The Man Torn Between His Desires

The Indian Rosewood features distinct layers, with a dark core and a light outer shell—another motif expressing inner conflict. The demon riding his left shoulder is clearly visible here. Height: 54 cm, Weight: 15 kg (excluding base).

The Man Torn Between His Desires

Here the angel on the right shoulder can be seen. The man covers his face with his hands in despair.

I represented the people of Israel as a dove of peace holding an olive branch in its beak. The Hamas snake emerging from its burrow bites her, and she pierces it with her sword. Carved into the base are the words: "I have no other land."

Creation vs. Evolution

Evolution is depicted here as two DNA molecules, with each sphere representing an atom. Primitive creatures like starfish, squid, and jellyfish emerge from the molecules, followed by insects, fish, dinosaurs, and mammals, totaling 20 animals. At the base, a man and a woman are in a birth position, with God's lightning striking each of their heads.

Creation vs. Evolution

The entire piece is carved out of a single block of wood. It took 14 months of dedicated work to complete.

Creation vs. Evolution

Finished with a protective layer of clear varnish.
